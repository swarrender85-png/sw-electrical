Home charging

For most households a charge point on the drive or garage wall is straightforward, but the detail matters: the position relative to where the car sits, the route back to the consumer unit, the protective devices required, and whether the existing board can take another circuit. I'll go through all of it with you before quoting.

Workplace and commercial charging

Workplaces install charge points for staff, fleet vehicles or visitors. The questions are the same but bigger: how many units, where they sit relative to the supply, and what the site can support without an upgrade. I can advise on what's realistic before you commit to a plan.

EV charging questions

Before you book

Do I need off-street parking?

For a home charge point, yes. The unit needs to reach the car where it is parked, on a drive, in a garage or on your own land. Running a cable across a public pavement is not something that can be signed off, so if you park on the street a home installation will not usually be possible.

Do I need planning permission?

In most cases no. A charge point on a house or in a garage normally falls under permitted development, though listed buildings and conservation areas can be different. Your electricity network operator does need to be told about the extra load, and I handle that notification as part of the job, so it's not something you need to arrange.

Where can the charge point go?

That depends on your parking, your incoming supply and the run of cable required. I'll look at the options with you before anything is fitted, and explain the trade-offs between the tidiest position and the most practical one.

Can you fit charge points at a workplace?

Yes. Workplace and commercial installations are covered as well as domestic ones, including multiple units where the supply allows.

Choosing a charger

Should I choose a tethered or untethered charger?

A tethered unit has the cable permanently attached, which is tidier day to day and means one less thing to carry. An untethered unit has a socket instead, so you use your own cable and it copes better if you change to a vehicle with a different connector. Neither is better in the abstract, it depends on how you use the car.

Which charger should I choose?

Ask me directly. Suitability depends on your vehicle, your supply and how you'll use it, so it's better answered for your specific situation than promised in advance on a web page.

Can I just use a three-pin plug socket instead?

It is not a sensible substitute. Charging from a standard socket draws a high current for many hours at a time, which is not what ordinary sockets and wiring are designed for, and it is considerably slower. A dedicated charge point is a properly protected circuit built for the job.

Timings

How long does an installation take?

Around three to four hours for a straightforward installation. It can take longer depending on where the charge point needs to go and how far the cable has to run, so I'll give you a realistic idea once I've seen the job rather than quoting a time blind.

How long will I wait from booking to installation?

Usually seven to ten working days for a standard booking. If you are claiming a government grant, allow the same again from the date your grant is approved, since the work cannot go ahead until that comes through.

The installation itself

Will my power need to be switched off?

Briefly, yes. Connecting a new circuit at the consumer unit means the supply comes off for a short period. I'll tell you before it happens so you can shut anything down that needs shutting down first.

Will I get certification for the work?

Yes. A charge point is a new circuit, so it is tested on completion and you get an electrical installation certificate. I'm NAPIT registered, so the work is notified through NAPIT as well.

What is a surge protection device?

An SPD protects an installation against voltage spikes, whether from lightning strikes on the network or switching events further up the supply. Given a charge point is a permanent circuit connected to an expensive vehicle, I fit surge protection as standard on EV installations rather than treating it as an optional extra.

What does OZEV registered mean?

OZEV is the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles. Being OZEV registered means I'm on the government's approved installer list for electric vehicle charge points.
